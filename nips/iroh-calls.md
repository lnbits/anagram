# Anagram Iroh calls v1

Calls support one-to-one audio/video and link-based group rooms. The thread header starts a call; the app-level panel provides accept, decline, microphone mute, camera controls, microphone/speaker selection, speaker mute, hangup, and minimization. The panel survives SPA navigation on desktop and mobile. Audio calls request only a microphone. Incoming calls do not request device access until accepted. A video invitation offers separate audio-only and video answers; audio-only acceptance requests no camera, while retaining the ability to enable it later.

## Private negotiation

An app-specific `kind:21117` **unsigned rumor** travels in a signed `kind:13` NIP-44 seal inside a `kind:1059` NIP-59 gift wrap. The existing decoder verifies the seal signature and binds the rumor's author to that signer. Controls have exactly one `p` tag for the intended Nostr account. They are never published unencrypted, copied to the sender, or inserted into message/contact/event persistence. Other clients can ignore this unassigned application kind. Room members exchange individual recipient-only controls; group-addressed rumors and self calls are excluded.

The JSON payload has `protocol: "anagram/iroh-call/1"`, a random UUID v4 `callId`, `action` (`invite`, `ringing`, `accept`, or `end`), `mode` (`audio` or `video`), and an ISO `expiresAt`. Invites and accepts also include `address: { id, relayUrl }` and `mimeType`. End signals include `reason` (`hangup`, `declined`, `busy`, `timeout`, `failed`, `cancelled`, or `unsupported`). Sender pubkeys come exclusively from the authenticated rumor author, never its JSON.

Rumors expire within 60 seconds of their creation; timestamps more than 10 seconds in the future are rejected. JSON, endpoint IDs, codec selection, recipients, and HTTPS relay URLs are validated. Only accepted chats or saved private contacts can ring; blocked/muted peers are suppressed. Duplicate, late, mismatched, multi-recipient, and group controls are ignored. A bounded per-account sessionStorage cache remembers recently handled call IDs across reloads without storing Iroh addresses or media. Device permission waits are cancellable from the call panel; late permission results are stopped.

The caller advertises a new Iroh endpoint, and the recipient explicitly accepts with its own new endpoint. The caller dials the endpoint authenticated by the recipient's DM. The recipient only accepts the endpoint authenticated by the caller's DM. Both exchange the call UUID over ALPN `anagram/call/1`; then both exchange a ready frame. Media capture may be previewed locally while dialing, but transmission starts only after this confirmed connection. The receiving client immediately sends a `ringing` acknowledgement, before requesting devices or waiting for acceptance. If no matching authenticated acknowledgement, acceptance, or end control arrives within 10 seconds after invitation publication, the caller ends negotiation and indicates that the client may not support calls or the peer may be offline. Acknowledged ringing still allows 60 seconds to answer; connecting allows 30 seconds. Endpoint creation has no persisted secret key, so calls never reuse an Iroh identity or connection.

## Media and lifecycle

The shipped Rust/Wasm module uses pinned Iroh 1.3.0. This browser transport operates through Iroh relays on web, Electron, and the Android WebView. QUIC/TLS encrypts the connection end to end, including when packets traverse a relay. Public address publication and lookup are disabled: endpoint addresses come exclusively from private Nostr negotiation. Browser Wasm cannot use native UDP hole punching; direct native transport would require a separate native binding.

Invites, acknowledgements and accepts advertise `mediaVersion: 2` and `videoSupported`. When both clients support version 2, each direction sends separate continuous WebM streams: Opus audio (`audio/webm;codecs=opus`) and optional VP8 video (`video/webm;codecs=vp8`). Audio capture starts without a camera in an audio call. Either participant can enable a camera later; disabling it stops the camera track and video recorder while audio continues. Video capture targets 640×360 at 24 fps, with 600 kbps video and 32 kbps audio. MediaRecorder emits segments at 100 ms intervals.

The authenticated, ordered QUIC stream carries big-endian u32 length-prefixed frames, capped at 1 MiB. Frame `0` carries audio, `1` is heartbeat, `2` readiness, `3` video, `4` an audio-container reset, and `5` followed by `0` or `1` disables/enables the remote video container. Clients additionally advertise `screenSupported`; only mutually negotiated screen-capable connections accept frame `6` (screen WebM data) and frame `7` followed by `0` or `1` (screen container off/on). Only negotiated version-2 connections accept frames 3–5. Control transitions are rate limited. Changing a microphone opens its replacement first, retains the existing mute state, cancels the old recorder's pending chunks, sends an ordered audio reset, and starts the replacement recorder. A failed acquisition leaves the existing microphone running. Old clients that accept within the 10-second discovery window retain the original combined WebM format; microphone switching and adding video to an audio call require both clients to update.

Heartbeats run every three seconds; 15 seconds without receiving a frame ends the call. Send and playback queues are bounded. Playback pruning advances monotonically, including when a browser retains a video keyframe after removal. The remote audio element stays mounted independently of camera changes, dialogs, minimization and routes, and is primed on the start/accept gesture. Playback refusal has a dedicated play-audio action and does not imply microphone permission denial. An obsolete play promise cannot clear the recovery action for a newer stream. Microphone/camera denial, missing/busy devices, output-selection denial, and transport/decoder failures have separate messages. Permissions API query results are never used to veto a device request.

Device menus use `enumerateDevices`, device constraints and, where supported, `setSinkId` / `selectAudioOutput`. The default speaker always remains available; browsers without output selection explain how to use system settings. Device IDs are held in memory and never included in Nostr controls. Device changes refresh the lists. Browser compatibility is determined through secure-context, Wasm, WebSocket, crypto, recorder, decoder and capture API capabilities before inviting the peer, rather than by user-agent strings. Unsupported browsers show a compatible-browser suggestion without negotiating.

Ending, cancellation, timeout, device failure, logout, or app unmount closes the connection/endpoint, stops all tracks and recordings, removes timers, and revokes playback URLs. Graceful end reasons also travel over QUIC. A refresh ends the call; calls are not resumed automatically. The same frame and negotiation protocol is used across web, Electron and Android; the browser/WebView supplies capture and output APIs.

## Screen sharing and presentation window

Screen sharing sends a third independent VP8 WebM stream, alongside microphone audio and optional camera video. Capture uses the browser's explicit screen/window/tab chooser (`getDisplayMedia`), targets up to 1920×1080 at 15 fps and 1.5 Mbps, and never starts from an incoming network request. It sends screen video; system/tab audio is not captured. Cancelling the chooser, stopping through the browser, or stopping from the call controls leaves the microphone and camera running. Late capture results after hangup are stopped.

The call panel fills the viewport and reserves space for controls. Shared screens and caller tiles fit the remaining space, side by side on wide displays and stacked on phones. The presentation window keeps camera and screen visible together. It mirrors decoded camera and screen frames into a same-origin popup using canvases. Live MediaSource elements remain attached to the main document, avoiding decoder detachment when opening or closing the window. Audio remains in the main app. Closing the popup leaves the inline presentation running. Incoming screens display inline until the user opens the presentation window, since remote messages cannot create popups with user activation. Popup blocking leaves an inline fallback. The main app must remain open.

Electron uses its native system picker where available and an explicit app picker otherwise. Source selections are bound to the requesting main frame, a random request ID and a short-lived source list; unrelated frames cannot select a source. Browsers/WebViews without `getDisplayMedia` can receive screens but cannot originate screen sharing. Android screen capture through a native MediaProjection service is not implemented.

## Group rooms

Use **Start or join a group call** in the new-chat menu (or the group-thread call button). This opens the call lobby in the current app window on desktop and mobile. Once connected, the maximized call dialog remains above the chat view; its bottom toolbar can minimize it so users can continue chatting. Start audio/video, then copy the invitation link. Room screen sharing stays in the call dialog; use the presentation-window control to open an additional view when wanted. Guests can open a web link or paste any app's link into the join screen and choose audio or video before capture begins. Web links keep the room capability in the URL fragment. Installed apps produce a portable `anagram://room/call/…` link for the paste-link action; OS-level registration of this link scheme is not assumed. Opening a web invite while signed out preserves the pending invite through login when session storage is available.

A room lasts while its host remains connected, with an eight-hour maximum lifetime and six participants. Anyone holding the link can join immediately; an accepted DM relationship is not required. Links contain the host pubkey, a random room UUID, a random 256-bit admission secret, bounded inbox relay hints and expiry. They contain no Iroh endpoint keys. Room controls use protocol `anagram/iroh-room/1` inside the same recipient-only `kind:21117` NIP-17/NIP-59 envelopes. They never create chats, messages, unread counts or notifications. A link sent by the user as an ordinary chat message is ordinary message content.

`join` carries the admission secret and the authenticated sender's member record (pubkey, per-join session UUID, display name, inbox relay hints). Only the host admits members and publishes monotonically numbered `roster` revisions. Each member validates host authority, its own membership/session and the participant limit. Blocked identities are refused. Rejoining creates a new session UUID so old departures and connection controls cannot affect the new join. Membership itself remains in memory.

Each participant pair establishes a separate fresh Iroh endpoint pair through nested call `signal` controls. Both sender and recipient session IDs are bound to that exchange, and the lexicographically lower pubkey initiates the pair to avoid simultaneous dialing. Only current host-authorized members can start media. Bounded short-lived buffering handles roster/control reordering. Camera and microphone are acquired once per participant and cloned into independent pair recorders. Screen capture is also acquired once after explicit user choice. Every pair has its own bounded queues, confirmation, heartbeat and teardown.

This is a full mesh, with no media server or host forwarding: upload/encoding costs increase with each participant. The six-person limit bounds this cost but does not guarantee performance on every device or network. Leaving releases that participant's media; remaining participants continue. The host's departure or loss of its Iroh connection ends the room. Room links are not persistent meeting rooms and cannot wake a closed app.

## Deployment and rebuilding

The generated JS/Wasm assets in `public/iroh/` are checked in and copied by all normal Quasar builds. Normal app builds do not require Rust. `manifest.json` records Iroh/wasm-bindgen versions and asset SHA-256 hashes. To change the transport, install Rust 1.91+, the `wasm32-unknown-unknown` target, Clang with Wasm support, and `wasm-bindgen-cli` **0.2.122**, then run `npm run build:iroh`. `WASM_BINDGEN`, `CARGO_HOME`, `CARGO_TARGET_DIR`, and the usual Cargo compiler environment variables can select local tooling/cache paths. Commit the Rust source, Cargo lockfile, and regenerated assets together. Preserve the bundled dependency licenses in `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.txt` and regenerate them from Cargo registry license files when upgrading dependencies.

By default all app targets use one shared pool containing `https://iroh.nostr.com/` and number0's four public Iroh relay hosts. Users choose Shared relay pool, Shared pool + my relays, or My relays only in Settings → Relays → Iroh relays (for calls). Built-in servers cannot be individually selected or entered as custom servers. The selected set is passed as a custom Iroh relay map for every new direct-call or group-room peer endpoint. Iroh selects its home relay from that set; it may also connect to the authenticated peer’s advertised relay. This is a home-relay preference, not a guarantee of equal traffic distribution. Before explicitly connecting to a peer-advertised relay, the shared transport guard allows only exact normalized URLs in the shared pool, deployment override, or locally selected relay set automatically. Other relays require an Allow once dialog; declining or ending the call prevents that connection. Approval is not persisted. Users can explicitly configure a custom relay in settings for future automatic connections, including LAN relays. This is a destination-consent policy, not DNS/IP filtering. Changes do not recreate existing endpoints. An explicit `APP_IROH_RELAY_URL=https://your-relay.example/` build override replaces the built-in pool for deployments and local tests. Provide browser-accessible HTTPS/WSS, the relay's HTTP probing endpoints, and sufficient capacity for sustained bidirectional media. Keep the generated module and Wasm file on the same origin and allow WebAssembly and relay WebSocket connections in any deployment CSP. Microphone/camera access requires a secure browser context.

Calls require MediaRecorder and MediaSource with WebM/Opus (and VP8 for video); unsupported engines show an actionable error. Current Chromium browsers, Electron, and current Android WebView are the primary targets. Android manifest permissions and macOS camera/microphone usage descriptions/entitlements are included. Incoming ringing follows browser audio autoplay rules. This implementation requires the app to be running: it does not provide Android background call wakeup, system dialer integration, or calls to offline peers. Physical device permissions, background/suspension behavior, and production relay capacity should be verified before release.

## Validation

`npm run test:e2e:local:calls-smoke` uses two local Nostr relays, the official pinned Iroh relay Docker image, a temporary local TLS proxy, and synthetic Chromium microphone/camera input. It additionally covers audio-only answers to video invitations, simultaneous camera/screen streams, receiver presentation-window rendering, and three-person link-based rooms with audible media in all six directions, camera upgrades, screen sharing and departure cleanup. It verifies audible synthetic audio in both directions and decoded video, microphone/speaker selection, cameras enabled during audio calls, camera teardown without audio interruption, browser capability rejection, busy-device diagnostics with granted input permission, blocked-playback recovery, mobile controls, ten-second no-response detection, minimization, SPA navigation, teardown, decline/cancel, and no chat-message pollution or replayed ringing after reload. The local test runner needs Docker, OpenSSL (`OPENSSL` can override its path), and Playwright's Chromium runtime dependencies. The full local suite includes these tests. Unit tests cover payload validation, authenticated ingest routing, publication, peer binding, races, replay, timeouts, and cleanup.

Browser behavior references: [Chrome autoplay policy](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/autoplay), [Audio Output Devices API](https://w3c.github.io/mediacapture-output/), and [MediaStream Recording](https://w3c.github.io/mediacapture-record/). A recorder's track set is kept stable for its lifetime; replacement starts a new container rather than modifying the stream being recorded.

## Direct call history

After a direct call invitation has been sent, the initiating client publishes one ordinary NIP-17 kind-14 message when the call ends. The recipient does not publish a second summary. Preflight failures (such as denied microphone access) and group-room connections do not create history. The message uses the existing persisted outbound delivery flow, including the sender's encrypted self-copy, so it can be restored like other chat messages. Abruptly closing or crashing the caller before finalization can leave no summary; this is not an authoritative call receipt.

Content is readable plain text, for example `Audio call · 1:05` or `Video call · Declined`. The inner rumor also carries the application-specific tag:

```json
["anagram-call", "1", "<call UUID>", "audio", "hangup", "65", "1"]
```

The fields after the version are call ID, original invitation mode (`audio`/`video`), end reason, connected duration in seconds (0–604800), and whether the connection became active (`0`/`1`). A call that never connected has duration zero. End reasons are `hangup`, `declined`, `busy`, `timeout`, `failed`, `cancelled`, and `unsupported`. These fields are inside the encrypted envelope; no media, device identifiers, or Iroh addresses are included. This tag is an Anagram extension, not a standardized NIP-17 call event. Unknown or malformed tags fall back to ordinary message text.

Anagram renders valid summaries in direct chats with direction, outcome/duration, and a phone button. Redial explicitly starts a new call to the current chat peer in the original invitation mode, with fresh Iroh endpoints. Receiving, restoring, or forwarding a summary never initiates a call. Forwarding uses plain fallback text; it does not carry call metadata. Call negotiation kind-21117 events remain hidden and separate.

## Private Iroh relay settings

The account's encrypted private preferences include `irohRelaySettings: { mode, customRelays }`, where mode is `pool`, `pool-custom`, or `custom`. Custom modes require at least one custom server. Up to 16 distinct custom HTTPS origins are allowed, without credentials, paths, queries, fragments or trailing-dot hosts. The built-in hosts are reserved for the whole pool, including when entered with different casing or ports. The selected mode resolves to at most 21 relay URLs for each new connection.

The default is the shared pool; the defaults button also removes custom entries. Choosing pool mode alone retains saved custom URLs but does not use them. Saving uses the existing self-encrypted account preference publication and restoration flow, independently of NIP-65 and app relays.

Peers advertising `mediaVersion: 2` and `muteStateSupported: true` in call negotiation exchange Iroh frame `8` with one byte (`0` unmuted, `1` muted). Send the current microphone state after the ready handshake and on each mute change, including each new group-call peer connection. Older peers receive no mute-state frames. This is participant-declared microphone state, not a measure of silence or speaker output volume.
