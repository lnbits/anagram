# Anagram Iroh calls v1

Calls are one-to-one, with audio or audio/video. The thread header starts a call; the app-level panel provides accept, decline, microphone mute, camera controls, microphone/speaker selection, speaker mute, hangup, and minimization. The panel survives SPA navigation on desktop and mobile. Audio calls request only a microphone. Incoming calls do not request device access until accepted.

## Private negotiation

An app-specific `kind:21117` **unsigned rumor** travels in a signed `kind:13` NIP-44 seal inside a `kind:1059` NIP-59 gift wrap. The existing decoder verifies the seal signature and binds the rumor's author to that signer. Controls have exactly one `p` tag for the intended Nostr account. They are never published unencrypted, copied to the sender, or inserted into message/contact/event persistence. Other clients can ignore this unassigned application kind. Groups and self calls are excluded.

The JSON payload has `protocol: "anagram/iroh-call/1"`, a random UUID v4 `callId`, `action` (`invite`, `ringing`, `accept`, or `end`), `mode` (`audio` or `video`), and an ISO `expiresAt`. Invites and accepts also include `address: { id, relayUrl }` and `mimeType`. End signals include `reason` (`hangup`, `declined`, `busy`, `timeout`, `failed`, `cancelled`, or `unsupported`). Sender pubkeys come exclusively from the authenticated rumor author, never its JSON.

Rumors expire within 60 seconds of their creation; timestamps more than 10 seconds in the future are rejected. JSON, endpoint IDs, codec selection, recipients, and HTTPS relay URLs are validated. Only accepted chats or saved private contacts can ring; blocked/muted peers are suppressed. Duplicate, late, mismatched, multi-recipient, and group controls are ignored. A bounded per-account sessionStorage cache remembers recently handled call IDs across reloads without storing Iroh addresses or media. Device permission waits are cancellable from the call panel; late permission results are stopped.

The caller advertises a new Iroh endpoint, and the recipient explicitly accepts with its own new endpoint. The caller dials the endpoint authenticated by the recipient's DM. The recipient only accepts the endpoint authenticated by the caller's DM. Both exchange the call UUID over ALPN `anagram/call/1`; then both exchange a ready frame. Media capture may be previewed locally while dialing, but transmission starts only after this confirmed connection. The receiving client immediately sends a `ringing` acknowledgement, before requesting devices or waiting for acceptance. If no matching authenticated acknowledgement, acceptance, or end control arrives within 10 seconds after invitation publication, the caller ends negotiation and indicates that the client may not support calls or the peer may be offline. Acknowledged ringing still allows 60 seconds to answer; connecting allows 30 seconds. Endpoint creation has no persisted secret key, so calls never reuse an Iroh identity or connection.

## Media and lifecycle

The shipped Rust/Wasm module uses pinned Iroh 1.3.0. This browser transport operates through Iroh relays on web, Electron, and the Android WebView. QUIC/TLS encrypts the connection end to end, including when packets traverse a relay. Public address publication and lookup are disabled: endpoint addresses come exclusively from private Nostr negotiation. Browser Wasm cannot use native UDP hole punching; direct native transport would require a separate native binding.

Invites, acknowledgements and accepts advertise `mediaVersion: 2` and `videoSupported`. When both clients support version 2, each direction sends separate continuous WebM streams: Opus audio (`audio/webm;codecs=opus`) and optional VP8 video (`video/webm;codecs=vp8`). Audio capture starts without a camera in an audio call. Either participant can enable a camera later; disabling it stops the camera track and video recorder while audio continues. Video capture targets 640×360 at 24 fps, with 600 kbps video and 32 kbps audio. MediaRecorder emits segments at 100 ms intervals.

The authenticated, ordered QUIC stream carries big-endian u32 length-prefixed frames, capped at 1 MiB. Frame `0` carries audio, `1` is heartbeat, `2` readiness, `3` video, `4` an audio-container reset, and `5` followed by `0` or `1` disables/enables the remote video container. Only negotiated version-2 connections accept frames 3–5. Control transitions are rate limited. Changing a microphone opens its replacement first, retains the existing mute state, cancels the old recorder's pending chunks, sends an ordered audio reset, and starts the replacement recorder. A failed acquisition leaves the existing microphone running. Old clients that accept within the 10-second discovery window retain the original combined WebM format; microphone switching and adding video to an audio call require both clients to update.

Heartbeats run every three seconds; 15 seconds without receiving a frame ends the call. Send and playback queues are bounded. Playback pruning advances monotonically, including when a browser retains a video keyframe after removal. The remote audio element stays mounted independently of camera changes, dialogs, minimization and routes, and is primed on the start/accept gesture. Playback refusal has a dedicated play-audio action and does not imply microphone permission denial. An obsolete play promise cannot clear the recovery action for a newer stream. Microphone/camera denial, missing/busy devices, output-selection denial, and transport/decoder failures have separate messages. Permissions API query results are never used to veto a device request.

Device menus use `enumerateDevices`, device constraints and, where supported, `setSinkId` / `selectAudioOutput`. The default speaker always remains available; browsers without output selection explain how to use system settings. Device IDs are held in memory and never included in Nostr controls. Device changes refresh the lists. Browser compatibility is determined through secure-context, Wasm, WebSocket, crypto, recorder, decoder and capture API capabilities before inviting the peer, rather than by user-agent strings. Unsupported browsers show a compatible-browser suggestion without negotiating.

Ending, cancellation, timeout, device failure, logout, or app unmount closes the connection/endpoint, stops all tracks and recordings, removes timers, and revokes playback URLs. Graceful end reasons also travel over QUIC. A refresh ends the call; calls are not resumed automatically. The same frame and negotiation protocol is used across web, Electron and Android; the browser/WebView supplies capture and output APIs.

## Deployment and rebuilding

The generated JS/Wasm assets in `public/iroh/` are checked in and copied by all normal Quasar builds. Normal app builds do not require Rust. `manifest.json` records Iroh/wasm-bindgen versions and asset SHA-256 hashes. To change the transport, install Rust 1.91+, the `wasm32-unknown-unknown` target, Clang with Wasm support, and `wasm-bindgen-cli` **0.2.122**, then run `npm run build:iroh`. `WASM_BINDGEN`, `CARGO_HOME`, `CARGO_TARGET_DIR`, and the usual Cargo compiler environment variables can select local tooling/cache paths. Commit the Rust source, Cargo lockfile, and regenerated assets together. Preserve the bundled dependency licenses in `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.txt` and regenerate them from Cargo registry license files when upgrading dependencies.

By default the client uses number0's public Iroh relay set. A deployment can set `APP_IROH_RELAY_URL=https://your-relay.example/` at build time to use its own Iroh relay. Provide browser-accessible HTTPS/WSS, the relay's HTTP probing endpoints, and sufficient capacity for sustained bidirectional media. Keep the generated module and Wasm file on the same origin and allow WebAssembly and relay WebSocket connections in any deployment CSP. Microphone/camera access requires a secure browser context.

Calls require MediaRecorder and MediaSource with WebM/Opus (and VP8 for video); unsupported engines show an actionable error. Current Chromium browsers, Electron, and current Android WebView are the primary targets. Android manifest permissions and macOS camera/microphone usage descriptions/entitlements are included. Incoming ringing follows browser audio autoplay rules. This implementation requires the app to be running: it does not provide Android background call wakeup, system dialer integration, or calls to offline peers. Physical device permissions, background/suspension behavior, and production relay capacity should be verified before release.

## Validation

`npm run test:e2e:local:calls-smoke` uses two local Nostr relays, the official pinned Iroh relay Docker image, a temporary local TLS proxy, and synthetic Chromium microphone/camera input. It verifies audible synthetic audio in both directions and decoded video, microphone/speaker selection, cameras enabled during audio calls, camera teardown without audio interruption, browser capability rejection, busy-device diagnostics with granted input permission, blocked-playback recovery, mobile controls, ten-second no-response detection, minimization, SPA navigation, teardown, decline/cancel, and no chat-message pollution or replayed ringing after reload. The local test runner needs Docker, OpenSSL (`OPENSSL` can override its path), and Playwright's Chromium runtime dependencies. The full local suite includes these tests. Unit tests cover payload validation, authenticated ingest routing, publication, peer binding, races, replay, timeouts, and cleanup.

Browser behavior references: [Chrome autoplay policy](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/autoplay), [Audio Output Devices API](https://w3c.github.io/mediacapture-output/), and [MediaStream Recording](https://w3c.github.io/mediacapture-record/). A recorder's track set is kept stable for its lifetime; replacement starts a new container rather than modifying the stream being recorded.
